# Everyday situations

The other pages explain each screen. This one starts from what happens in real life and shows how
to record it so the budget stays right.

## Starting in the middle of the month {#mid-month}

There is no need to enter the past. Create your accounts with their **current balance** as of
today: that money lands in Ready to Assign. Assign it to what is still to come this month, and
record transactions from today on. Next month you will have a full month of history.

## Income that varies {#irregular-income}

When income changes from month to month (freelance work, commissions), budget only the money that
has already arrived, never what you expect. Assign the essentials first: housing, bills, food.

Once you get ahead, leave some money in Ready to Assign at the end of the month, or keep it in a
category such as "Next month". What is left in Ready to Assign carries into the next month, so you
can start each month by assigning money you already earned.

## Bills that come once a year {#annual-bills}

Car tax, insurance, a yearly subscription: give each one a category with a **Save up** goal **by
the month** it is due ([Goals](budgeting.md#goals)). Moneta tells you how much to set aside each
month, and **Fund goals** in Quick assign does it in one tap. When the bill comes, the money is
waiting in the category.

## A refund or a return {#refund}

Record the money that comes back as an **inflow in the category of the original purchase**, not in
an income category. The category gets the money back, as if the purchase never happened. An income
category would add it to Ready to Assign instead.

## Expenses shared with someone {#shared-expenses}

You paid a dinner of 120 and your friend owes you 60. Enter it as a [split](transactions.md#splits):
60 in Dining Out and 60 in a category such as "Owed to me". Assign money to cover both. When your
friend pays you back, record the 60 as an inflow in "Owed to me": that category returns to zero.

## Taking out cash {#cash}

Have a **Cash** account, and record the withdrawal as a [transfer](transactions.md#transfers) from
checking to cash. It doesn't touch the budget: the money just changes accounts. Categorize what
you spend from the cash account, as with any other account.

## A credit card that already has debt {#existing-card-debt}

When you add the card, enter the **amount owed**. By default it goes to Starting Balance and comes
out of Ready to Assign, which may go negative if the debt is larger than the money you have.

To pay it off over time instead, choose a category of its own as the **starting balance
category**, such as "Old card debt", and turn on [Roll overspending over](budgeting.md#carryover)
in it. The debt then sits there as a negative balance, and each month you assign what you can
afford to pay it down. New purchases on the card take money from their own categories as usual.

## Paying the credit card bill {#card-bill}

Record the payment as a transfer from checking to the card. As long as your purchases were
recorded with categories that had the money, the money for the bill is there: it was set aside when
you bought. See [Credit cards](accounts.md#credit-cards).

## A loan payment with interest {#loan-payment}

Keep the loan as a tracking account, with what you owe. Each month:

1. Enter the whole payment as a transfer from checking to the loan, in a category such as "Car
   loan": the money leaves the budget.
2. If your bank says how much of it was interest, enter that interest on the loan account as an
   outflow, so its balance matches what the bank says you still owe.

## Moving money to savings {#savings}

A transfer between two budget accounts, from checking to savings for example, doesn't change the
budget. What you save is in the categories you assigned it to, like an Emergency Fund.

## When a category runs out {#category-runs-out}

Spending more than a category had is not a mistake to hide: [cover it](budgeting.md#overspending)
by moving money from another category. That is the point of the method: you choose what gives way,
instead of the overspending coming out of next month without you noticing.
